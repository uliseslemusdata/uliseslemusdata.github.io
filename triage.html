The problem

Small businesses lose leads to slow response times. Research from Harvard Business Review and MIT, along with more recent industry studies, consistently shows the average business takes over 40 hours to respond to a new inquiry, while leads contacted within 5 minutes are dramatically more likely to convert than those contacted even 30 minutes later. Business owners are busy running their operations, not watching an inbox.

What I built

Triage is a fully custom lead qualification system that runs live on this site's contact form. When someone submits a message, it's automatically analyzed by AI for intent, urgency, and fit, classified, and routed accordingly, all within seconds.

AI Qualification Webhook Architecture CRM Integration Spam Detection n8n Automation

Architecture

Custom HTML form, built and styled to match this site, embedded directly in the contact section
Form submits to an n8n Webhook, not a third-party hosted form
Claude analyzes the lead for intent, urgency, and specificity, and classifies it as a sales lead, spam, or other
A parsing layer structures the AI's output
Every lead is logged to a HubSpot CRM contact record and a backup spreadsheet, regardless of classification, so nothing is ever lost
Qualified, high priority leads trigger an instant email notification, so the business owner only gets interrupted when it matters
The lead receives a personalized, human sounding acknowledgment, drafted by AI based on their specific message, not a canned template

A real world proof point

Spam submissions like this aren't rare. In my own work in property management, foreign language promotional spam and phishing attempts show up regularly on lead capture forms across the industry, often with no filtering catching them before they land as "new leads."

One recent example: a submission containing foreign language promotional content and a clearly fabricated name had been accepted as a legitimate lead on a widely used property management platform's contact form.

I ran the same submission through Triage. It was correctly flagged as spam in seconds, with a clear, specific reason.

Triage's classification
"Mixed language content, suspicious email domain, no company name, incoherent message mixing an unrelated tour request with foreign promotional text."

Tested across a range of spam patterns, not just one

Catching a single spam example is easy to dismiss as luck. To confirm Triage generalizes, I ran it against several distinct spam patterns, each with a different manipulation strategy.

  • Foreign language promotional spam (the case above): flagged for mixed language content, a suspicious domain, and an incoherent message
  • Advance fee / prize scam: a fake $500,000 prize notification requesting bank details, flagged as a classic advance fee scam with fake prize notification, urgency tactic, and request for banking details
  • Low effort generic spam: an unsolicited "get rich quick" pitch from a temporary email address, flagged as unsolicited promotional messaging with no legitimate business inquiry and a temporary email address typical of spam

In every case, Triage didn't just flag the message, it explained why, citing the specific signal that gave it away. That's meaningfully different from keyword based spam filters: it's reasoning about intent, not just pattern matching against a blocklist.

This same qualification logic was also tested across legitimate lead types, including high urgency prospects, low intent browsers, partnership inquiries, and multilingual messages, all classified correctly.

Engineering details worth noting

  • Honeypot spam protection: a hidden form field invisible to real users but often filled by bots, silently filtered before it ever reaches the AI or wastes an execution
  • Deliberate response timing: the response to the website visitor waits until qualification is complete, so the confirmation they see is meaningful, not instant and empty
  • Redundant logging: even with a full CRM integration, every lead is also backed up to a spreadsheet, so no data is ever dependent on a single system
  • Tuned tone: the AI's draft responses were deliberately refined through iteration, moving from generic, marketing sounding replies to responses that read like a real person casually responding to an email
